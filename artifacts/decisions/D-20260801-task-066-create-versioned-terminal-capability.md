# D-20260801 — Create a Versioned Terminal Capability

Date: 2026-08-01
Status: accepted by TASK-066
Disposition: `CREATE VERSIONED TERMINAL CAPABILITY`

## Context

TASK-064 established complete editorial closure and an exact seven-row paired-table contract, while expressly not proving accepted-profile or renderer compatibility. TASK-065 then stopped in Phase 1 before Candidate authoring because the accepted TASK-062 profile, strict schemas, projection and renderer cannot carry that contract.

## Decision

Create one additive, explicitly versioned terminal capability. Do not revise TASK-064's governed editorial representation to fit historical limits. Do not mutate TASK-062 or any historical profile, schema, renderer, evidence or source closure.

Frozen capability family:

- profile: `briefforge:representative-paired-terminal-static-html-profile:3.0.0`;
- projection schema: `briefforge:representative-paired-static-html-projection-schema:1.0.0`;
- renderer: `briefforge:representative-paired-static-html-renderer:1.0.0`;
- conformance profile: `briefforge:representative-paired-static-html-conformance-profile:1.0.0`.

## Why this route is necessary

No complete Route A mapping can preserve all exact statement identities, hierarchy, one-to-many qualification targets, obligations, provenance, value metadata, row ownership and five-column realization. Several blockers are structural and independent of prose volume. The unchanged renderer alone makes Route A impossible by producing fourteen slot rows with different headings.

Route B adds only the missing bounded semantics. Existing BCRP eleven-kind abstract capacity, lifecycle model, operational-authority separation and historical routes remain sufficient.

## Rejected alternative

`REVISE EDITORIAL REPRESENTATION CONTRACT` is rejected. It would require at least one forbidden compromise: merge mandatory identities, erase target-specific edges, encode typed metadata in unvalidated prose, split governed obligations/provenance into altered identities, or accept fourteen value-slot rows in place of seven relationship rows. Lower item counts would not prove equivalence.

## TASK-064 qualification

TASK-064 remains reconciled for:

- immutable source-content closure;
- 109-item editorial ledger;
- exact paired-table contract;
- abstract eleven-kind BCRP representability;
- pre-promotion gate and TASK-063 counterfactual.

Its `existing capacity is sufficient` conclusion means generic BCRP semantic capacity only. It does not mean accepted-profile admission, projection preservation, renderer realizability or complete terminal-route compatibility.

## Exact successor authority

A separately activated TASK-067 may implement only the frozen versioned capability in the accepted TASK-066 specification, add focused/adversarial tests and prove backward compatibility. It may add one fail-closed exact version tuple where required. It must stop before a representative Candidate.

## Explicitly unauthorized

No Candidate/Judgment/Ready/Release, renderer invocation, artifact, semantic-conformance evidence, Manifest, delivery, deployment, TASK-056 evidence, migration of historical records, generic renderer selection, broad refactor, new BCRP kind, network/database/browser work or successor activation is authorized here.

## Coexistence and retirement

The capabilities coexist. There is no automatic migration, reinterpretation, alias, fallback or retirement. Any future deprecation requires a separate evidence-backed decision; old persisted evidence must remain independently verifiable indefinitely under its original exact tuple.

## Consequences

Positive: preserves governed meaning, enables a truthful terminal route, and isolates compatibility risk.

Cost: coordinated profile/schema/projection/renderer/conformance and dispatch work is larger than a prose edit. This is necessary complexity because the contradiction spans those layers.

Risk control: bounded capacities, fixed schema and renderer, exact dispatch, no domain IDs in production, independent conformance and historical byte/source-closure tests.
